## 2. Dataset and task

Exact factorial: **9 monomer A × 682 monomer B × 7 composition/architecture cells = 42,966 rows**.
Targets `EA_vs_SHE_eV` and `IP_vs_SHE_eV`. Source `data/ea_ip.csv`.

Variance decomposition (`_dataset_design_audit.md` §0.2):

- A identity + B identity ≈ 0.93 (EA) / 0.90 (IP) of total variance
- architecture *within* fixed (A, B, fracA) ≈ **0.98% (EA), 1.46% (IP)**

So chemistry dominates and architecture is ~1% of the signal. The comparison that matters is
between architectures at fixed (A, B, fᴀ), where the chemistry term cancels.

**Two-axis metric decomposition.** Group-mean R² (chemistry placement) versus ΔR² and pairwise
ordering (architecture recovery within a group). Both reported always.

**Null-floor predictor.** A-blind / B-blind group-mean lookup table — no parameters, no fitting.
Skill score `(R²_model − R²_null) / (1 − R²_null)` = `1 − MSE_model/MSE_null`. Every group-mean R²
must appear beside its fold-specific null floor. On the A split the EA metric is near-degenerate:
null median group-mean R² **0.676** on EA versus **−0.034** on IP, and the null *beats the model*
on one EA fold.

## 3. Models

| key | description |
|---|---|
| `wdmpnn` | weighted D-MPNN baseline (Aldeghi & Coley style) |
| `hpg_hier` | two-stage hierarchical: Stage-1 atoms→monomer, Stage-2 2-node transition graph, stoichiometry-weighted readout |
| `hpg_hier_octamer` | Stage-2 replaced by an 8-slot explicit sequence + attention pooling |
| `hpg_hier_junction` | junction coupling — message passing across A/B before pooling |
| `hpg_hier_junction1` | 1-step junction variant; **R1 only, by design** |

Never regenerated under the fixed protocol and therefore not usable: the Q1 "wedge" variant,
ChemArch, GlobalArch, frac, the fusion ablation.

### Octamer mechanics (verified in source)

- `_build_octamer_sequences` enumerates all `C(8, n_A)` arrangements. **Non-uniform transition
  matrix → argmax, a single sequence. Uniform → sample `n_random_samples` (16) with replacement.**
- `run_hpg_generalization.py:403` — `octamer_rng_seed=args.seed`, **not** hard-coded 42. Seeds
  42/43/44 each draw a different set of 16 sequences, so the error bars already cover
  sequence-draw variance, and it is still the most stable model.
- `OctamerPathLayer` docstring: bidirectional path message passing **with no edge features**. The
  octamer therefore *discards* the 16-d port-pair edge feature the baseline carries
  (`_stage2_edges` builds `pairs = np.zeros((2, 2, PORT_COUNT * PORT_COUNT))`).
- The 16 replicas are averaged **inside the loss**: forward returns `pred_sum / replica_counts`
  and `_loss` takes MSE against that mean. The octamer is *trained* as a 16-member ensemble with
  a variance-reduced gradient. This is the leading explanation for its low seed SD.

## 4. The model-selection bug and the fixed protocol

**Bug.** All five runners, since first commit, wrote predictions from the *final* patience-expired
model rather than the best checkpoint. Cost ≈ 0.048 eV MAE for HPG-hier. Every pre-fix
single-seed result is invalid and was withdrawn.

**Run-to-run variance.** Three identical runs — same model, seed, split, code, GPU — on EA fold 1
gave group-mean R² 0.450 / 0.790 / 0.978, **SD 0.268** (`_noise_floor_results.md`, fold-1
`group_mean_r2_sd = 0.26783125`). Single-run benchmark numbers are not measurable at this scale.

> Corrected 30 July. This was previously quoted as SD 0.091, which is the fold-1 **MAE** SD
> (`mae_sd = 0.09106691`, in eV) and cannot be the SD of three unitless R² values. See §9.

**Frozen protocol now in force.** Best-checkpoint predictions; **three seeds (42/43/44) averaged
at the prediction level** as the replicate unit; `--frozen_protocol` guard. Adopting it improved
24 of 24 metrics.

**Statistics.** Paired per-fold sign tests, Holm correction. With 9 folds the minimum attainable
two-sided p is 0.0039; within S/D groups of 5 and 4 it is 0.0625 and 0.125. **Nothing reaches
significance.** The paper reports consistency and effect size and says so explicitly.

## 5. Splits

**R1 — A-heldout (`monomer_heldout`).** 9 folds, one A monomer held out each.

**R3 — B-heldout clustered (`monomer_b_heldout_clustered`).** 9 folds over the 682 B monomers,
capacity-balanced Murcko scaffold packing (Bemis & Murcko 1996, via RDKit).

B space is dominated by two scaffold families: 112 Murcko scaffolds, the largest 317 (46%) and
109 (16%) — **62.5% of all B monomers**. A balanced scaffold-disjoint split is therefore
impossible, and **the nine folds are not exchangeable**.

Hence the derived fold grouping, computed in `analyze_regen_v1_r3.py`:

```python
composition["fold_group"] = np.where(
    composition.same_scaffold_share > WITHIN_SCAFFOLD_SHARE_THRESHOLD,
    "S_within_scaffold", "D_cross_scaffold")
```

- **S (within-scaffold)** — folds 0–3: 76 of 76 held-out monomers have a same-core relative in training.
- **D (cross-scaffold)** — folds 4–8: folds 7–8 have 0 of 75. Folds 4 and 7 still reach max Tanimoto 0.95.

**Never pool the nine B folds.** Report S and D separately. Group D is 5 folds, 4 excluding the
scaffold-homogeneous fold 5.

## 7. Claim 8 is confounded five ways

Baseline Stage-2 versus octamer Stage-2 differ in all of:

| # | factor | HPG-hier | octamer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | topology | 2 nodes | 8-slot chain |
| 2 | positional embeddings | – | 8 learned vectors |
| 3 | readout | stoichiometry-weighted sum | attention pooling |
| 4 | edge features | 16-d port-pair + transition weight | **none — a loss** |
| 5 | replicas (random rows only) | 1 | 16, averaged inside the loss |

### The 2×2 that resolves factors 1 and 3

| | stoich / mean readout | attention readout |
|---|---|---|
| **2-node graph** | HPG-hier — *have* | **arm C** — runs today |
| **8-slot chain** | **arm D** — needs a patch | octamer — *have* |

Pre-register the reading: D ≈ octamer and C ≈ baseline → the **sequence** did it. C ≈ octamer and
D ≈ baseline → the **readout** did it. Both midway → they interact, do not attribute. Both ≈
baseline → it was factor 2, 4 or 5.

**On an 8-slot chain the stoichiometric readout *is* mean pooling over positions**, because
`n_A = round(8 · fᴀ)` means the slot counts already encode composition. It is the exact analogue
of `f_A·h_A + f_B·h_B`, not a substitute. Pooling happens after message passing, so arrangement
information survives; only the learned weighting of positions is removed. Use exactly one readout
variant — adding max/sum/gated turns attribution into a search.

**Arm D does not exist and fails silently.** `OctamerEncoder` is instantiated only when
`stage2_mode == "octamer_sequence" and stage2_readout == "attention"` (`hpg_hier.py:201-203`), and
`data/hpg_hier.py:37` hard-codes `+ 2 * polymer_idx`. Passing
`--stage2_mode octamer_sequence --stage2_readout stoich_weighted` today runs the **exact baseline
model** with no error. Add an assertion regardless of whether arm D is built.

**Factor 5** is tested by `--n_random_samples 1`, no code change. It changes the training
objective, not just inference — do not describe it as "fewer test-time samples". It bites only on
**random-arrangement rows**; report that subset separately. Pre-registered prediction: if seed SD
jumps but ΔR² holds, the stability was the ensembling and the architecture claim survives.

**Factors 2 and 4 stay confounded** inside the 8-chain arm even after the 2×2. State as a limitation.

## 8. Protocol parity failure — found 29 July, unresolved

The models were **not** trained under a matched protocol. From `resolved_config` in the sidecars
(not `cli_args`, where wDMPNN's module constants appear as null):

| | wDMPNN | HPG family |
|---|---|---|
| batch size | **512** | **64** |
| epoch cap | **300** | **100** |
| LR | chemprop scheduler | flat Adam 1e-3 (`hpg_hier.py:163,282`) |
| num_workers | 4 | 0 |
| patience | 15 | 15 — *the only field `--frozen_protocol` guards* (`run_wdmpnn_generalization.py:435`) |

Consequences:

1. **Epochs-to-best is not comparable across the batch boundary.** In gradient updates wDMPNN
   takes **0.38×** what HPG-hier does — fewer, not more. Any claim that wDMPNN "converges more
   slowly" is an artifact of an 8× larger epoch. Deleted from all outputs.
2. **What survives: 134 vs 115 s per epoch.** An epoch is one pass over identical data, so this is
   a fair unit — and wDMPNN is slower per pass *despite* 8× larger batches and 4 dataloader
   workers against HPG's 0.
3. **Claim 7 — the headline — is confounded.** wDMPNN may be under-tuned at batch 512 with a
   different LR schedule rather than architecturally worse. No runner records `init_lr` or
   `max_lr`, so this cannot be settled from the sidecars.
4. One octamer cell is **undertrained**: R1, IP, fold 2, seed 43 — best at epoch 97 of a 100 cap,
   still improving. Exclude or flag it. Every other capped run is wDMPNN well inside its 300.

**A batch-64 wDMPNN arm is now the highest-value outstanding run**, above the attribution ablation.

## 11a. Checkpoint selection is near-uninformative about ΔR²  (found 30 July)

Within each (model, target, fold) cell, does the seed with the lower `best_val_loss` have the
better test metric? Over all pairs of runs:

| | picks better group-mean R² | picks better **ΔR²** | median within-cell SD: group-mean R² vs ΔR² |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 (262 pairs) | 58% | **63%** | 0.0152 vs 0.0609 — **4.0×** |
| R3 (214 pairs) | 56% | **56%** | 0.0054 vs 0.0277 — **5.2×** |

Coin flip is 50%. Validation MSE is barely better than chance at identifying the checkpoint with
better architecture recovery, and ΔR² is 4–5× noisier than group-mean R². The reason is structural:
architecture is ~1% of total variance (§2), so val MSE is ~99% chemistry and ΔR² rides along as an
uncontrolled by-product of which epoch the stopper happened to land on.

Fold 0 shows it directly — seeds 42/43/44 give group-mean R² 0.958 / 0.975 / 0.990 (tight) while
ΔR² goes 0.805 / 0.568 / 0.670. The seed with the second-best chemistry placement has the worst
architecture recovery.

This also explains the octamer's lowest-median / highest-max seed-SD profile: in-loss replica
averaging smooths the gradient and therefore the trajectory, so the by-product is more consistent —
except when it isn't.

**Consequence: noisy, not biased.** It does not invalidate existing results; it bounds what they
can claim. See §12a.

**Do NOT switch checkpoint selection to a val-ΔR² criterion as a fix to this campaign.** It would
invalidate all 481 runs (~26 kSU), and selecting the maximum of a quantity with SD ~0.06 over ~100
epochs invites winner's curse with no guarantee test ΔR² improves. It is a future-paper question.

**Determinism, corrected.** `run_hpg_generalization.py:258` and `:260` are the *same expression*
(`torch.backends.cudnn.deterministic`), and `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms` is never called
outside `tests/_check7_deterministic.py` and `tests/_check7_zero_grad.py`. So
`deterministic_requested=True, deterministic_algorithms_enabled=False` is a **misleading field
name, not a silent fallback** — there is no fallback event to investigate. Fixed-seed runs still
diverge (0.45 vs 0.98 on fold 1); `scatter_add_` atomics (`hpg_hier.py:13`) remain the likely
mechanism, but the log does not evidence it. The fixable share of variance cannot be estimated from
2 folds × 3 repeats — it needs repeats, not arithmetic. Free actions: rename the field; run
`use_deterministic_algorithms(True, warn_only=True)` once to see what it reports.
